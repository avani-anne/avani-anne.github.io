Undergraduate Researcher — FICUS Lab, PI Sarah Kavassalis

The FICUS Lab's research focuses on flexible, interdisciplinary computational approaches for understanding the atmosphere. I studied the interpretability of long short-term memory models (LSTM) used to predict ozone levels, and evaluated the explainable-AI technique layerwise relevance propagation (LRP). I processed and restructured air quality datasets, tuned LSTM hyperparameters to find the optimal settings for ozone prediction, and analyzed LRP results across multiple model initializations and diurnal and seasonal cycles, comparing them against random forest baselines using Pandas and SHAP.
Undergraduate Researcher — Gerbode Lab for Soft Matter Physics, PI Sharon Gerbode
2022–2024
The Gerbode Lab studies the fundamental mechanisms that influence aging in small crystals, and I focused on grain boundaries — a property that determines many bulk material properties, with applications in materials like photovoltaics. I developed scripts analyzing cooperative motion, vorticity, and misorientation angle during granule dissolution, and found that the size of rotating granules was inversely related to misorientation angle, echoing the underlying Moiré pattern in the crystal lattice. I also identified cooperative motion, where particles follow each other as they move, as a signature of dissolution, and built ramp cells using optical blasting to create grain boundaries for physical observation. I co-authored a peer-reviewed APS publication on this work and presented it at the APS March Meeting 2024.
